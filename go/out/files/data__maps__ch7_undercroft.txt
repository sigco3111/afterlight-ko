Cages. Small ones — child-sized. Every door hangs open. Whatever was inside left, or was let out, or was used.
A drowned order of service, the ink run to violet: "VESPERS EVE. The Under-Choir shall descend to the Cistern and hold the Reflected Note until the Precentor's signal. Do not stop for any reason. — By order of the Hierophant."
Hymnals, swollen fat with water. One has fallen open to a page where somebody has written in the margin, very small: _I don't want to sing this one._
A reliquary statue of a Keeper holding up a lantern. Something large has rubbed against it so often that one side is polished to a shine.
A little statue of Saint Aurel, patron of tuners, still dry. Someone left a candle stub at her feet. It is not very old.
A pillar carved with a high-water mark from two hundred years ago. The water today is three hands higher.
A great bronze bell, sunk to its shoulder. Its rim is engraved: _I CALL THE LIVING. I MOURN THE DEAD. I BREAK THE LIGHTNING._ It has done none of these things in a year.
Vestry shelves. Choir robes hang in rows, dripping. There are exactly as many empty hooks as there are singers in the Loft.
A bellwright's anvil, carried all the way down here for repairs. The tools are laid out neatly, as if the smith only stepped away.

41	Every flower here is a last word. I keep the book of names that goes with them. Someone must remember them, or what are they for?
42	There was a calling for this, once. Mnemonists. Keepers of memory. The Choir struck it out when it decided some things were better forgotten.
43	The last Mnemonist is buried at the heart of the maze. The Choir set something on her grave — an appetite. It eats names. It has eaten so many.
44	If you can stand before it and still remember who you are, the calling is yours. I would like someone to remember her.
45	Sexton Mabry at the Garden's gate says the last Mnemonist lies at the heart of the Garden's maze, where the Forgetting guards her grave.
46	The heart of the maze. Hold on to your name.
47	You came back with your names. All of them? Good.
48	...And hers. I can feel it. Someone remembers her now. That is all a grave is for.
49	A plain stone at the heart of the maze. The name on it has been eaten away. Around it the flowers are silent — and something pale is feeding on the quiet.
50	The Forgetting turns toward you, and for a moment you cannot remember your own name. Then Wren says it out loud, and so does everyone else.
51	The Forgetting comes apart into a thousand small sounds — names, voices, the last words of everyone it ate, rushing back into the flowers. The name on the stone returns: ISOLDE VANE, MNEMONIST. Crest-glass clears in the stone around a Crest like a knot with no end.
52	ISOLDE VANE, MNEMONIST. The flowers around her stone are murmuring again.
53	In the Garden That Remembers we stood before the Forgetting and kept our names. The last Mnemonist has hers back, and the calling is ours.

1	You've the look of Lanternfall, girl. Salt in the eyes.
2	My gran was a Tidecaller. You'll not have heard of it. The Choir struck it from the rolls when the fishing towns stopped paying tithe — said the sea was a heathen thing to sing to.
3	Struck it out of the books, but they couldn't strike it out of the water. Gran said the last Tidecaller's crest-glass went down with the last singer, out past the strait. She's still out there, singing. I hear her some nights.
4	If the sea's gone, maybe the song is all that's left of it. Go and listen. Take it back, if she'll let you.
5	Grandmother Wend in Keelhaven says the last Tidecaller's crest-glass lies at the far east end of the Strait That Was, still guarded by its singer.
6	Past the last bridge, at the very end of the strait. Listen for her.
7	You found her. I heard it last night — the singing stopped. Thank you for letting her rest.
8	Sing to the sea sometimes. It's still listening, wherever it went.
9	A slab of crest-glass, crusted white with old salt, wedged in the rock like a figurehead. Something inside it is singing.
10	Water that is not there rises around the glass — and in it, a drowned woman in a Cantor's robe, still singing the boats home.
11	The song ends on a long, low note, like a tide turning. The crest-glass clears, and in it burns a Crest none of you has ever seen: three waves, each over the last.
12	The crest-glass is clear and quiet now. If you listen very hard, it still sounds a little like the sea.
13	At the end of the Strait That Was, the Drowned Cantor sang over a lost Crest. We let her finish her song. The Tidecaller calling is ours to carry now.
14	Hask. Last of the founders' guild. Well — last of anything, these days.
15	Every bell in this city was poured by Bellwrights. That was a calling once, child — a Crest. Then the Choir decided a bell that could call the faithful could call other things too.
16	They struck the calling from the rolls and walled the guild's crest-glass up in the old foundry under the Undercroft, with the Abbot to watch it. The Abbot is a bell. Don't ask.
17	The flood opened the walls. If you're going down there anyway... ring it for me. Once. That's all I've ever wanted.
18	Founder Hask in the ruins of Aurenne says the Bellwrights' crest-glass is walled up in the far north-east of the drowned Undercroft, watched by the Bronze Abbot.
19	North-east corner of the drowned Undercroft, past the old reliquary. You'll hear it before you see it.
20	I heard it. All the way up here, through the ash. First time in four hundred years that bell has rung for anyone but the Choir.

21	Go on, then, Bellwright. Make some noise.
22	A bell of crest-glass, taller than a man, hangs in a cage of rusted chain. Before it, something bronze stirs on its yoke.
23	The Bronze Abbot swings down from the dark, ringing with every step it takes.
24	The Abbot cracks down the middle with a sound like the last bell of evening. The chains fall away. In the glass bell burns a Crest you have never seen: a bell, mid-swing, ringing.
25	The glass bell hangs free now. Touch it, and it hums.
26	Under Aurenne we rang the bell the founders hid. Hask heard it. The Bellwright calling is ours now.
27	Kiteholm flies kites because kites are what we have. But there were folk who flew more than kites, once. Kitewrights.
28	The Choir struck them out. "Only the sun belongs in the sky." Burned the workshops. One didn't burn — it was already too far down to reach.
29	It's in the Underhang. Deep. The south end. With the thing they built to lift it, which never lifted anything but grudges.
30	If you find their crest-glass, bring the calling back up. Let somebody fly who isn't a kite.
31	Ada Kestrel in Kiteholm says the Kitewrights' last workshop lies at the far south end of the Underhang, guarded by the Scrap Colossus.
32	South, as deep as the Underhang goes. Mind the lines.
33	Look at you. A Kitewright. Gaffer Quill is going to be unbearable about this — he always said they'd come back.
34	Go on. Fall upward.
35	A workshop hangs in the dark, all struts and old canvas, half-swallowed by the rock. At its heart, a slab of crest-glass shaped like a wing.
36	Something huge unfolds out of the scrap around the glass, rivets screaming.
37	The Colossus sags, then sits, almost gently, as if it had only ever wanted to be allowed to stop. The wing-shaped glass clears. In it burns a Crest: a kite on a long line.
38	The wing of crest-glass catches the faint light from above. The Colossus sits beside it, very still, keeping it company.
39	Deep in the Underhang we found the Kitewrights' last workshop, and the Colossus they built to lift it. The Kitewright calling flies again.

1	Old Gnarlhide
2	the East Fields of Thistledown
3	A boar the size of a hay cart has been tearing up the East Fields outside Thistledown. The farmers call him Old Gnarlhide and leave him the south field.
4	A boar the size of a hay cart, gray with age, rooting through the stubble. Its tusks are as long as Wren's arm. It lifts its head and looks straight at you.
5	Old Gnarlhide goes down like a felled oak, and the field is quiet.
6	The Wold Widow
7	the heart of the Whisperwold
8	In the Whisperwold's heart there's a web strung between two oaks, wide as a sail. Whatever spun it is called the Widow, and her brood comes with her.
9	A web between two oaks, wide as a sail, beaded with dew and worse. Something the color of a bruise sits at its center. It has far too many eyes, and all of them open.
10	The Widow curls up like a burnt leaf. Her web sags and tears in the wind.
11	Mother Knot
12	the sewers under Aurenne
13	The rat-catchers won't go into the deep sewers under Aurenne anymore. They say there's a rat king down there, a hundred rats knotted at the tail, and it eats its own young to heal.
14	A heap in the dark that squeaks with a hundred voices: rats knotted tail to tail into one great shuddering thing. The middle of it has a face.
15	Mother Knot comes apart into a hundred fleeing rats. None of them look back.
16	Glasseye
17	the deep dunes of the Sunscar
18	Pilgrims crossing the Sunscar talk of a basilisk with one eye of clear glass. Look into it and your legs turn to stone, they say. Look away and it breathes fire.
19	A long, low drake basks on a dune of fused sand. One of its eyes is clear glass with the sun burning in it. The air around it shimmers.
20	Glasseye's glass eye cracks across. Then the light goes out of it.
